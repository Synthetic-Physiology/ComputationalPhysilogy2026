<h2 style="color: #1d5fa7; font-weight: 600; letter-spacing: 0.3px; margin-bottom: 10px;">
Interactive Modeling of the Time-Dependent Modulation of Aquagenic Pruritus by Beta-Alanine
</h2>
<p>
This project was created as part of the course <em>Computational Physiology</em> and investigates how beta-alanine affects aquagenic pruritus and paresthesia depending on dose, intake timing, and water-contact duration. Aquagenic pruritus is a rare yet distressing form of itch triggered by brief skin contact with water without visible skin lesions.
</p>
<p>
Reports from affected individuals and documented cases show that antihistamines often offer little relief, whereas timely beta-alanine intake before water exposure can markedly reduce or suppress the itch. Because literature and quantitative time-series data on individual cellular steps remain limited, the model uses dynamic relations in arbitrary units (<em>a.u.</em>) rather than fixed absolute values, serving as a didactic, hypothesis-driven teaching model.
</p>
<p>
The browser-based application features three graphs and shows how intake amount, timing before water exposure, and contact duration modulate signaling along the cascade.
</p>
